# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Saudi e-commerce merchants (solo operators and small teams) running stores on Salla and Zid. They import products from AliExpress and CJDropshipping, manage wallets, and rely on automated fulfillment to run their businesses without constant manual oversight. They check their dashboard on desktop during work hours, scanning order status and wallet balance to confirm everything is flowing. Arabic-first audience with English as a secondary language; RTL layout is a first-class requirement.

Secondary user: the platform SuperAdmin (SaaS owner) who manages merchants, approves bank transfers, configures commission/subscription tiers, and monitors system health across the entire platform.

## Product Purpose

DropLinker automates the dropshipping workflow for Saudi merchants: connecting their Salla/Zid stores to global suppliers (AliExpress, CJ), syncing inventory, auto-fulfilling orders from a prepaid wallet, and providing AI-assisted product content. Success looks like a merchant who opens the dashboard, sees green across the board, and closes it knowing their business ran itself overnight.

## Brand Personality

Effortless. Precise. Trustworthy.

The voice is calm and competent, never salesy or breathless. It communicates like a senior operations manager who already handled everything before you asked. Numbers speak louder than decoration. Status is conveyed through clarity, not through visual noise.

## Anti-references

- **AliExpress Seller Center / Cluttered wholesale dashboards.** Dense, overwhelming, zero hierarchy. Every element screams for attention. DropLinker is the opposite: quiet confidence, clear priority.
- **Generic WordPress admin panels.** Flat, utilitarian, zero craft. DropLinker should feel intentionally designed, not assembled from default components.
- **"AI-generated" aesthetics.** Gradient text on dark backgrounds, glassmorphism on every surface, neon accents, hero-metric cards with big numbers and small labels repeated in grids. If someone could screenshot it and say "AI made this," it has failed. Every visual choice must be deliberate and earned.
- **Overly playful SaaS tools.** Cartoon illustrations, bouncy animations, emoji-heavy copy. DropLinker's merchants are managing real money; the interface should respect that.

## Design Principles

1. **Clarity over decoration.** Every element exists to communicate, not to impress. If removing something doesn't reduce understanding, remove it.
2. **Status at a glance.** A merchant should know the health of their business within 2 seconds of opening any screen. Hierarchy, color coding, and spatial grouping do the work.
3. **Quiet until important.** The default state is calm. Color, motion, and visual weight are reserved for things that actually need attention: errors, pending actions, successful milestones.
4. **Respect the operator.** These users manage real inventory and real money. The interface treats them as competent professionals, not as onboarding targets. No hand-holding, no unnecessary confirmation dialogs.
5. **Bilingual by design.** Arabic RTL is not an afterthought bolted onto an English layout. Spacing, alignment, and reading flow work natively in both directions.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA compliance. All interactive elements must be keyboard-navigable. Color is never the sole indicator of status (pair with icons or text labels). Support `prefers-reduced-motion` for users who disable animations. Full RTL support for Arabic with mirrored layouts, not just flipped text.
